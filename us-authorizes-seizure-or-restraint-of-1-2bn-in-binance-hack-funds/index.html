US authorizes seizure or restraint of $1.2bn in Binance hack funds

The Justice Department says court-authorized seizure or restraint now covers more than $1.2 billion of Binance hack proceeds, while tracing and forfeiture efforts continue.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 13, 2026 · 14:51 UTC·3 min read

US authorities have obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, while government and private-sector efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion in total. The Justice Department’s announcement says the work to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets is ongoing; authorization to seize or restrain funds does not establish that forfeiture is complete.

The theft occurred on or about October 29. The FBI attributes it to North Korean military cyber actors it calls TraderTraitor. The Justice Department describes the stolen amount as more than $6 billion; the Associated Press reports it as about $6.3 billion. That makes the $1.2 billion court-authorized amount a portion of the stolen assets, not a final accounting of what the government has recovered.

What the figures count

  • More than $6 billion stolen: the Justice Department’s figure for assets taken from Binance.
  • More than $2.7 billion frozen: the combined total from US government and private-sector efforts since the theft.
  • More than $1.2 billion authorized for seizure or restraint: the portion for which the Justice Department says it has obtained court authorization.

The figures describe different stages. A freeze restricts movement of assets; court authorization permits the government to pursue seizure or restraint through legal process. Neither figure by itself means a court has issued a final forfeiture order or decided who is entitled to the assets. The Associated Press report notes that the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines whether anyone has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

Tracing across bridges, mixers and exchanges

The Justice Department says TraderTraitor actors continue to launder the stolen assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. These services can move assets between networks, pool transactions or swap one asset for another, complicating efforts to follow the funds and identify where intervention is possible. The department says law enforcement teams have worked with virtual asset service providers to interdict the assets, but it does not detail which providers froze which amounts or identify the specific mechanisms used in each freeze.

The FBI’s October 31 alert says investigators observed the actors converting stolen assets primarily into Ether and expected further laundering through decentralised finance and instant-swap services. The FBI alert publishes addresses that it says hold or have held assets from the theft and asks service providers, including DeFi services, instant exchanges and node operators, to block transactions with or derived from those addresses. The alert describes an operational request to intermediaries; it does not report a change to a blockchain’s consensus rules or a reversal of confirmed transactions.

The next stage is both investigative and legal. Authorities are still tracing the funds and pursuing seizure and forfeiture, while the published addresses give participating services identifiers to screen against. The announcements leave open how much of the frozen total will ultimately be forfeited, whether victims or other parties will establish claims, and how much remains outside authorities’ reach.
