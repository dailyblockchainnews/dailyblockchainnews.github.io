Fairshake backs 32 House candidates as CLARITY Act stalls

Fairshake named 32 House candidates for midterm support, including six receiving $1 million each, as the Senate’s CLARITY Act stall leaves market rules unresolved.

The Hashbeam Desk·Oct 12, 2026 · 09:33 UTC·3 min read

Fairshake plans to support 32 House candidates in November’s midterms, putting crypto campaign spending behind lawmakers from both parties as legislation to set federal digital-asset market rules remains stalled in the Senate. The slate has 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats; six candidates are each slated for $1 million in spending, according to The Associated Press’s report on Fairshake’s announcement.

The six are Democrats Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California, and Republicans French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan. Fairshake did not disclose the planned spending for the other 26 candidates, so $6 million is the disclosed commitment to six races, not a stated total budget for the full slate.

What is Fairshake committing to spend?

Fairshake has announced election spending to support candidates, but the announcement does not establish that the money has already been spent. Roll Call’s account of the announcement identifies Hill as chair of the House Financial Services Committee, Steil as chair of its Digital Assets, Financial Technology and Artificial Intelligence Subcommittee, and Huizenga as the committee’s vice chair. The six disclosed allocations cover three Democrats and three Republicans.

The group’s backing also extends to House Democratic Caucus Chair Pete Aguilar, Republican Majority Whip Tom Emmer, Republican Conference Chair Lisa McClain and House Ways and Means Committee Chairman Jason Smith. Fairshake spokesperson Geoff Vetter said the group backs “pro-crypto candidates” in both parties. He cited Fairshake and its affiliated groups’ spending in primaries and special elections this year: $40 million supporting 29 Democrats and $31 million supporting 28 Republicans. Those figures concern earlier contests, not the announced House slate.

What legislation links the candidates?

All 32 candidates supported the CLARITY Act, the market-structure bill that passed the House in July 2025 and then stalled in the Senate. The bill aimed to establish rules for digital-asset markets and divide oversight between federal regulators. Senate Democrats blocked a procedural vote in September; the vote was 49-50, and the measure did not advance.

The disagreement included ethics provisions. Democrats objected that the bill did not adequately address President Donald Trump’s crypto investments and sought stronger safeguards. The procedural defeat left the industry’s market-structure legislation unresolved; it did not itself enact or change regulatory rules. Fairshake’s slate therefore backs lawmakers who supported the House bill, while leaving the Senate negotiations and any future legislation unsettled.

How large is the wider election effort?

The House slate is one part of Fairshake’s broader campaign spending. The group announced plans in September to spend nearly $30 million against Democratic Senate candidate Sherrod Brown in Ohio, who is challenging Republican Sen. Jon Husted. AP reported that Fairshake and affiliated PACs had about $120 million in cash on hand at the end of August. Fairshake has not said whether it will spend more in House or Senate races before November.

The six $1 million commitments make the announced House spending concrete, but the unknown allocations for the remaining candidates limit what can be inferred about the group’s full deployment. The slate spans both parties, yet its stated common criterion is support for legislation the crypto industry favors. Whether that support translates into a revived market-structure bill will depend on the next Congress and the Senate votes needed to move legislation forward.
