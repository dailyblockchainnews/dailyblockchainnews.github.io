EU targets crypto networks in proposed Russia sanctions

The Commission’s 22nd Russia sanctions package would target crypto and payment networks, including third-country enablers, alongside new bank and sports restrictions.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 27, 2026 · 02:20 UTC·3 min read

The European Commission’s proposed 22nd sanctions package would extend financial restrictions on Russia to crypto and payment networks, including in third countries accused of helping Russia evade sanctions. The Associated Press report on the proposal says the package must be endorsed by all 27 EU member states before it can take effect; that process can take weeks, and measures may change.

In her statement presenting the 22nd package on 23 November, Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the EU would extend a transaction ban to about 60 Russian banks and remove those banks from SWIFT, the messaging system used for international payments. She also said the package would target crypto and payment networks in Russia and third countries that help Russia evade sanctions. The Commission has launched a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support that work.

The statement does not name crypto platforms, tokens or chains, or specify the legal test for identifying a third-country service that facilitates sanctions evasion. It also does not set out a transaction-level screening rule or describe how the task force will operate. The confirmed scope is therefore a proposed crackdown on crypto and payment networks, with third-country enablers included; the operational details remain unspecified in the announcement.

The bank measures are more specific: a transaction ban would cover roughly 60 Russian banks, and all 60 would be removed from SWIFT. These are two distinct restrictions in the Commission’s description. The proposed transaction ban limits dealings involving the listed banks; removal from SWIFT cuts their access to the financial messaging network. The Commission’s statement does not identify the banks or provide an implementation date. Both measures remain part of a package awaiting approval by member states.

The package also proposes expanding an existing ban on European energy services so European companies could not help Russia build, operate or repair energy infrastructure, including refineries. For the first time, the Commission says, the services ban would cover electricity services. It proposes listing more than 70 additional ships associated with Russia’s shadow fleet and banning tanker vessels insured in Russia. Those are separate measures from the crypto provisions, but they sit within the same sanctions proposal.

Other proposed restrictions would bar Russian teams and athletes from competing under the Russian flag at sports events in the EU, prohibit the display of official Russian symbols at sports and cultural events, and restrict financial flows from Russian sponsors to European sports and cultural associations. The Commission also proposes visa restrictions for Russian ex-combatants and rules preventing Europeans from holding posts on governing bodies of companies in Russia. Von der Leyen described the package as a way to increase pressure on Russia; its stated intent does not establish the effect the measures would have if adopted.
