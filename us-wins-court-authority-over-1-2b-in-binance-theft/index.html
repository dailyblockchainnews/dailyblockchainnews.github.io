US wins court authority over $1.2B in Binance theft

The Justice Department says courts authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion in stolen Binance assets, part of $2.7 billion frozen so far.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 14, 2026 · 08:17 UTC·3 min read

The Justice Department says it has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a legal step aimed at keeping those assets available while investigators pursue recovery. The Justice Department’s November 12 announcement says the amount is part of a larger pool frozen through cooperation with private-sector firms.

The distinction matters: a freeze blocks movement through participating services, while seizure or restraint places assets under legal control or preserves them for proceedings. The department has not said the $1.2 billion has been forfeited to the United States or returned to victims. The Associated Press report says that, typically, the government files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

Three figures mark different stages of recovery

  • More than $6 billion stolen: The Justice Department says members of the North Korean military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, took more than this amount in virtual assets from Binance on or about October 29. The FBI attributes the theft to North Korea; that is the government’s assessment.
  • More than $2.7 billion frozen: This is the total the department says US government and private-sector efforts have frozen so far. It includes the assets covered by the court authorization; it is not an additional recovery figure.
  • More than $1.2 billion authorized for seizure or restraint: This is the portion of the frozen total for which the department says it has obtained court authorization. The announcement does not identify the tokens, wallets, courts, or specific orders that make up that amount.

These amounts describe different points in the process. The reported theft measures assets taken; the freeze total describes assets prevented from moving; and the court-authorized amount describes assets subject to legal seizure or restraint. The Justice Department says its teams worked with virtual asset service providers (VASPs) to interdict funds. It does not publish a transaction-by-transaction account of how the providers froze or transferred the assets, so the announcement does not establish that every dollar in the authorized amount is now held in a government-controlled wallet.

Tracing continues as funds move through services

Investigators say the recovery effort is ongoing. The Justice Department reports that TraderTraitor actors continue to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers, and exchanges. The FBI’s October 31 alert says it observed the actors converting stolen assets primarily into Ether and expected further laundering through decentralized finance and instant-swap services. The FBI urged VASPs and infrastructure operators to block transactions from, or derived from, addresses it identified as connected to the actors.

That request targets the points where laundering activity may meet services able to screen or stop transactions. It does not reverse settled blockchain transfers by itself. The government’s stated approach combines tracing on public ledgers with cooperation from VASPs, then uses court authority to seize or restrain assets that remain reachable. The agencies have not published the detailed tracing path for the funds in the $1.2 billion figure, so the precise mix of on-chain controls and provider actions remains undisclosed.

The next legal question is ownership, not simply custody. As AP reports, a forfeiture proceeding gives potential claimants a route to ask a judge to recognize their interest in recovered property. Until that process is resolved, court authorization to seize or restrain assets does not by itself say who will ultimately receive them. The Justice Department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize, and forfeit the Binance funds continue.
