Kim Yo Jong denies North Korean role in Binance theft

Kim Yo Jong rejected the FBI’s attribution of Binance’s $6.34 billion theft to North Korea, while Binance’s account describes unauthorized use of its wallet signing service.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 5, 2026 · 11:38 UTC·3 min read

Kim Yo Jong, the sister of North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, denied North Korea’s involvement in the Binance theft and called the FBI’s attribution “a trite provocation,” escalating a dispute over who carried out a $6.34 billion loss. The Associated Press report on her statement says she accused Washington of linking North Korea to cybercrime whenever major incidents occur. Her denial does not address the FBI’s technical evidence publicly: the bureau attributed the theft to the North Korean activity it calls TraderTraitor and published cryptocurrency addresses it says are operated by or connected to those actors.

What evidence has the FBI disclosed?

The FBI says the theft occurred on or about October 29, 2026, and that investigators observed stolen assets being converted primarily into Ether. It expects further laundering through decentralized finance and instant swap services. Its notice lists addresses holding or previously holding stolen assets and urges service providers to block transactions involving them. The public notice states the bureau’s attribution, but it does not publish a complete forensic account explaining how investigators identified the operators.

Binance’s November 3 incident report gives a separate account of the transaction path. It says attackers gained unauthorized access to an internal wallet signing service and initiated transfers outside the normal withdrawal process. Between 20:05 and 20:09 UTC on October 29, five hot wallets sent 233.46 million XRP to an attacker-controlled address; Binance also reports transfers of about 970,500 SOL, 44.2 million OP and 18,270 BTC. These are Binance’s reported findings, and the company says its investigation is ongoing.

How did EIP-7702 feature in the theft?

Binance says the attacker used Ethereum’s EIP-7702 authorization mechanism through the compromised signing service. EIP-7702 lets an account delegate specified execution rights to a smart contract without revealing the account’s private key. In this incident, Binance says, the authorization gave an attacker-controlled contract partial execution rights over a hot wallet. That describes a misuse of the authorization path; Binance has not said that EIP-7702 itself was defective.

The company reports that the final unauthorized transfer, 2,961.9 ETH, finalized on-chain at 23:56 UTC after remaining pending. At 00:10 UTC on October 30, Binance executed a new authorization from the Binance14 hot wallet to the zero address, clearing the EIP-7702 delegation code. The Binance incident update with its initial technical findings says no further unauthorized transfers were observed after the final transfer.

The scale remains disputed only in attribution, not in the basic incident figures reported by the company and investigators. Binance puts the impact at approximately $6.34 billion and says it had frozen or intercepted about $2.85 billion by its November 3 update, subject to reconciliation and price volatility. It also says it had reserved more than the loss amount to cover affected assets. Those are company-reported response figures; they do not establish who controlled the attacker’s addresses.

Kim Yo Jong’s statement rejects the FBI’s conclusion but offers no competing account of the wallet transfers or the use of EIP-7702. The FBI’s public attribution and North Korea’s denial therefore remain distinct claims while Binance says its investigation and independent review are continuing.
