FBI ties $6.3 billion Binance theft to North Korea

The FBI attributes Binance’s reported $6.3 billion theft to North Korea’s TraderTraitor group and says stolen assets are moving into Ethereum before laundering.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 1, 2026 · 11:43 UTC·3 min read

The FBI has attributed the theft of more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance to North Korea, saying the actors are converting the proceeds primarily into Ethereum before attempting further laundering. In an October 31 alert, the FBI identified the activity as TraderTraitor and asked crypto services to block transactions linked to addresses it listed.

The alert dates the theft to on or about October 29. Binance put the estimated impact at about $6.3 billion, according to Associated Press reporting. The FBI’s attribution is an investigative finding; Binance’s estimate describes the reported financial impact. The bureau says the same TraderTraitor actors were behind last year’s $1.5 billion Bybit theft.

What access path has Binance identified?

Binance’s initial investigation points to a potentially compromised DevOps engineer laptop that may have let an attacker access a service container and submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions. The company said it had found no evidence that a wallet private key was leaked. Those are preliminary findings from Binance; the FBI alert does not specify the initial access method, the service involved, or how a withdrawal was authorized.

That leaves a key technical detail unresolved: public statements identify a suspected route into Binance’s systems, but do not describe the transaction approval path or any exploited code. They do not establish a flaw in Ethereum or another blockchain protocol. Binance said it would publish more technical detail after completing its investigation and verifying the facts.

How are investigators tracking the stolen assets?

The FBI says it and its partners at the U.S. Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation have observed TraderTraitor converting the stolen assets primarily into ETH. It expects the group to use decentralized finance services and instant swap services for further laundering. The alert does not say that all stolen funds have been converted or specify how much remains recoverable.

The agency published Ethereum addresses it says are holding or have held assets from the theft and are operated by, or closely connected to, TraderTraitor. It urged THORNode and RPC node operators, instant exchanges, DeFi services, blockchain analytics firms and other virtual asset providers to block transactions with or derived from those addresses. These requests target identifiable on-chain flows; they do not themselves reverse confirmed transfers or guarantee that downstream assets can be frozen.

What is known about customer losses and recovery?

Binance said it had worked with blockchain partners to freeze roughly $3 billion on-chain, and that it had enough of the same digital assets set aside to cover 90% of the missing funds. The company promised full reimbursement to customers, AP reported. Those figures describe the exchange’s statements; the FBI alert does not confirm a final loss total or the amount ultimately recovered.

The investigation therefore has two distinct tracks: determining how unauthorized withdrawals passed through Binance’s internal systems, and tracing assets as they move across blockchains and services. The FBI’s address list gives intermediaries specific identifiers to screen, while Binance’s public account of the suspected access route remains preliminary. Neither statement yet supplies a complete transaction-by-transaction reconstruction.
