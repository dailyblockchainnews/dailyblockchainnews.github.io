Excluding a Bridge Cuts Routes, Sometimes at a Price

What does excluding a bridge in aggregator settings do? It removes every route that uses that bridge, which can improve your risk fit but leave fewer, pricier options.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 28, 2026 · 06:54 UTC·4 min read

Excluding a bridge removes every route that uses it from an aggregator’s quotes, so you avoid that bridge at the cost of fewer, sometimes pricier routes. The router searches only the bridges still allowed by your settings; it does not keep the same route and swap out the excluded bridge automatically.

Excluding one bridge can remove more than one route

Take a USDC transfer where the best quote uses Stargate. Exclude Stargate, and its routes vanish from the results; the next-best available quote moves up. That may mean a higher cost, a different route, or no quote at all for the pair.

The filter applies to each leg of a route. A route may combine a bridge hop with another bridge hop or a swap. If Stargate appears anywhere in that chain, excluding it removes the whole route, including its other legs. This is why the route count can drop more than expected: one bridge filter may knock out several combinations, not just the route that prominently names that bridge.

LI.FI’s API supports bridge allow and deny parameters, which constrain the protocols considered during route discovery. An aggregator’s settings use that kind of constraint to narrow the router’s search; the exact controls depend on the interface. See the LI.FI API documentation for its routing parameters.

Fewer options do not guarantee a safer transfer or a better quote. They change which routes can be proposed. The remaining routes still have their own fees, execution conditions, and trust assumptions, and the quote can change as those inputs change.

Exclude a bridge for a clear risk reason

Excluding a bridge is useful when you have a specific reason to avoid its design or trust model. Bridges validate and move assets in different ways, so one user may be unwilling to accept assumptions another user accepts. L2BEAT’s bridge risk material describes trust assumptions as something to assess across bridge designs, rather than treating all bridges as interchangeable.

Consider two users making the same USDC transfer between the same networks, with the same amount and quote conditions. The first distrusts a bridge’s design and excludes it, accepting that the best remaining quote may cost more. The second sees no specific risk concern and excludes the same bridge only to shave a small amount off the displayed cost. The filter removes the same routes for both users, but only the first has a clear reason to accept the narrower choice set.

The benefit is control over route eligibility: the excluded bridge will not appear in a route the router offers under that filter. The cost is losing every otherwise usable route that depends on it, including a route that might have been the cheapest or fastest for that transfer. Too many exclusions can leave no available route for a particular token and network pair.

For a Jumper bridge user who normally accepts the defaults, treat an exclusion as a change to the search space, not a quote optimization switch. Jumper bridge uses route selection to determine which supported bridge or combination can carry a transfer, so excluding one bridge can change the options shown for your specific pair. Recheck the route and the full quote after changing settings.

  • Risk preference: Excluding a bridge you distrust removes routes that depend on it.
  • Price: The next eligible route may cost more if the excluded bridge supported the lowest quote.
  • Speed: Removing a route can also remove the fastest available option.
  • Availability: A long deny list can leave no route for the selected pair.

Excluding a bridge differs from excluding an exchange

Excluding a bridge filters cross-chain transfer protocols; excluding an exchange filters venues or services used to swap one asset for another. A route can involve both: an exchange may handle a token swap while a bridge carries assets between networks. Filtering one category does not automatically filter the other, though removing either can disqualify a combined route.

How do I use only one specific bridge on Jumper bridge?

Use a bridge allowlist if the settings expose one, and allow only the bridge you want. A denylist works in the opposite direction: it names bridges to remove, so every other eligible bridge can remain in consideration. With only one allowed bridge, routes requiring another bridge on any leg will be dropped, and the pair may have no available route. Review the resulting route before proceeding.
