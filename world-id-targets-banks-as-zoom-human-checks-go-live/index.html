World ID targets banks as Zoom human checks go live

World introduced a World ID package for financial firms and made its Zoom human check generally available, extending its proof system into institutional workflows.

The Hashbeam Desk·Oct 19, 2026 · 07:31 UTC·3 min read

World unveiled World ID for banking and finance at Money20/20 in Las Vegas on Oct. 18, packaging its identity app, AI-agent tools and Zoom integration for financial institutions. The Block’s report on the launch says World ID for Zoom also moved from a limited beta, which began in April, to general availability. The offering gives firms ways to request cryptographic proofs about people behind digital interactions; it does not establish that a bank has adopted the system.

What does the banking suite ask users to prove?

World says the app can provide three kinds of proof: Proof of Human, Proof of Presence and Proof of Attributes. Proof of Human establishes that someone is a real, unique person after a one-time visit to an Orb. Proof of Presence is a phone-based liveness check. Proof of Attributes verifies selected information from an official government credential, such as age or nationality, and reveals only the requested attribute, according to World’s announcement of the financial-services suite.

The proofs correspond to different checks, rather than one universal identity record. World says an institution could request Proof of Human for a high-stakes action, Proof of Presence to establish that a user is present, or an attribute proof for an age- or nationality-based requirement. The company describes possible uses such as branch interactions, large transfers, account opening, transfer approvals and account recovery. These are described as deployment scenarios, not evidence that banks have implemented them.

World named Bain Capital, OpenExchange, Outtake, Pantera Capital and VanEck Funds as design partners. The Block reported that World did not identify a bank using the suite or disclose pricing. Design-partner status therefore confirms work exploring the technology, not a live banking rollout.

How does the Zoom check work?

A participant first verifies their World ID at an Orb and connects the World ID app to their Zoom account. During a call, the integration uses the app and a live selfie to check that the person on screen matches the Orb-verified person associated with the account. If authenticated, a Human Badge appears on the participant’s Zoom tile. World says Zoom receives no personal information during that process.

This is an identity check tied to a verified person; the announcement does not describe it as a detector that scans video for synthetic artifacts. World ID for Zoom’s general availability follows the limited beta, The Block reported. World says meeting hosts will be able to require Proof of Presence in a waiting room, while a participant can request a check during a call. The company’s wording describes the host-controlled waiting-room option as forthcoming.

What can an AI agent present, and what remains in beta?

World ID for Agents, also called AgentKit, lets a person delegate selected proofs to an autonomous agent. The agent can then present those proofs to a third-party platform to show that it acts on behalf of a real human, while withholding that person’s private information, according to World. AgentKit remains in beta, so the banking announcement does not establish general availability for agent-based financial transactions.

World says more than 18 million people hold a Proof of Human. That existing credential base is the foundation the company is offering to institutions, but the launch leaves adoption and integration details unresolved: no bank customer was named, and the agent tools remain in beta.
