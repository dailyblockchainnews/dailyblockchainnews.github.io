US secures seizure authority over $1.2B in Binance theft

The Justice Department says court orders cover more than $1.2 billion of Binance theft proceeds, within $2.7 billion frozen as authorities trace laundering routes.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 17, 2026 · 09:12 UTC·3 min read

U.S. authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, while the government and private-sector partners have frozen more than $2.7 billion tied to the theft. The Justice Department’s November 12 announcement describes the $1.2 billion as part of the larger frozen total, not as funds already returned to victims.

  • More than $6 billion stolen: The FBI attributes the October 29 theft to North Korean military members it calls TraderTraitor. The figure measures the reported theft, not the amount now under U.S. control.
  • More than $2.7 billion frozen: U.S. agencies and private-sector partners have interdicted funds since the theft. A freeze restricts movement; it does not establish who will ultimately receive the assets.
  • More than $1.2 billion covered by court authorization: The Justice Department says it has obtained authority to seize or restrain this portion of the frozen funds. The announcement does not say that all of it has been forfeited or returned.

What the seizure authority covers

The distinction between freezing, seizing and forfeiting matters here. The Justice Department says law enforcement and virtual asset service providers (VASPs) worked together to interdict the stolen assets. It does not identify the specific wallets, tokens or service providers holding the $1.2 billion, or describe a single technical procedure used to immobilize those assets.

The legal authority is to “seize or restrain” the specified cryptocurrency. That wording covers government action against assets, but the announcement does not say every token was transferred into government-controlled wallets. Nor does it describe a blockchain protocol change, contract upgrade or consensus-level reversal. The disclosed mechanism is coordinated action by law enforcement and VASPs, backed by court authorization.

The department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the stolen assets are continuing. TraderTraitor actors are still laundering funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, according to the announcement. The FBI has urged VASPs to block transactions with, or derived from, addresses the group uses to launder Binance proceeds. That instruction targets activity visible to or handled by service providers; the public notice does not claim that every route or address has been blocked.

Forfeiture remains a separate step

Seizure authority does not settle ownership or guarantee repayment. The Associated Press report notes that the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The Justice Department’s announcement does not report that such a final determination has been made for this cryptocurrency.

The scale of the freeze is substantial relative to the reported theft, but the totals describe different stages: more than $6 billion stolen, more than $2.7 billion frozen, and more than $1.2 billion subject to court-authorized seizure or restraint. The remaining funds are not accounted for in the announcement. Authorities say tracing and enforcement work is ongoing as the laundering routes remain active.
