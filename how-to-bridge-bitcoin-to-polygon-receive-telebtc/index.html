How to bridge Bitcoin to Polygon: Receive teleBTC

To bridge Bitcoin to Polygon, deposit BTC through TeleSwap and receive teleBTC at a Polygon address after verification; swap separately if you want another token.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 27, 2026 · 23:39 UTC·4 min read

TeleSwap bridges BTC to Polygon by verifying a Bitcoin deposit and minting teleBTC to your Polygon address. Bitcoin and Polygon cannot transfer assets directly, so the bridge connects them through Bitcoin transaction verification on the EVM side and collateralized Lockers.

Bitcoin deposits become teleBTC on Polygon

For a BTC-to-teleBTC route, the bridge links a Bitcoin transaction to a Polygon token mint. An on-chain Bitcoin light client verifies the deposit on the EVM side, and Lockers provide collateral for teleBTC issued against deposited BTC. The result is a teleBTC balance on Polygon that you can hold or swap. Readers weighing routes can also see how TeleSwap compares with other Bitcoin options in a comparison of TeleSwap routes.

The bridge quote and a later token swap are separate steps. Receiving teleBTC completes the bridge; exchanging it for another Polygon asset adds a decentralized exchange trade. That trade’s output depends on the pool’s depth and the price impact of your swap, so a quoted bridge output does not tell you how much of another token you will receive.

Before starting, have BTC in a self-custody Bitcoin wallet and a Polygon receiving address. You will also need a way to pay Polygon gas if you plan to move the teleBTC after it arrives. The receiving address is where the minted token should be sent, so review it carefully when the request details are shown.

Deposit BTC, wait for verification, then confirm the mint

Say you want to move BTC into a Polygon wallet and keep the Bitcoin exposure represented by teleBTC. Start by selecting BTC as the input and Polygon as the destination in the bridge interface, then connect the relevant Bitcoin and Polygon wallets. This sets the route and provides the address information needed for the request.

Next, review the destination address and the quoted teleBTC output, along with the request details. Confirm that Polygon is selected and that the address is the one you control. The quote describes the bridge route; it does not include a later exchange into a different Polygon token.

Approve the request in the interface and send the requested BTC from your Bitcoin wallet to the designated Locker. The Bitcoin deposit is now the input for the bridge. Keep the request details available so you can identify the transaction associated with your route while it is being verified.

The bridge verifies the Bitcoin transaction on the EVM side using its on-chain Bitcoin light client. Once the deposit is accepted, the Polygon contract mints teleBTC to the specified address. The task is complete when you confirm the teleBTC balance in that Polygon wallet.

At that point, you can hold teleBTC on Polygon or make a separate swap. If you want another asset, review the exchange route and its expected output independently: pool depth and slippage affect the trade. A thin pool or a large trade relative to available liquidity can change the amount received, even though the bridge already delivered teleBTC.

Related

What do you need to bridge BTC to Polygon?

You need a Bitcoin wallet with BTC, a Polygon receiving address, and a way to pay Polygon gas if you plan to move the teleBTC after it arrives.

Can you bridge Bitcoin to Polygon as WBTC?

The bridge output is teleBTC. To get WBTC, you need a separate supported swap route on Polygon, and its output depends on the available trading pool and the trade’s price impact.

Once teleBTC appears in your Polygon wallet, decide whether to keep it or compare a supported swap’s output before trading. That leaves you with either Bitcoin exposure on Polygon or the other asset you chose, with the bridge and any exchange treated as distinct transactions.
