DOJ gets authority to seize $1.2 billion in Binance theft

The Justice Department says court authorization covers more than $1.2 billion of Binance theft proceeds, within $2.7 billion frozen as tracing and forfeiture continue.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 23, 2026 · 07:51 UTC·3 min read

The U.S. Justice Department has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, while efforts to trace and forfeit the assets continue. The department said the theft was carried out on or about October 29 by North Korean military members tracked by the FBI as TraderTraitor, according to the Justice Department’s November 12 announcement.

The figures describe different stages of recovery, not a single pool of funds that has already been returned to victims:

  • More than $6 billion stolen: the Justice Department’s stated value of virtual assets taken from Binance.
  • More than $2.7 billion frozen: the combined result of U.S. government and private-sector efforts since the theft.
  • More than $1.2 billion subject to court-authorized seizure or restraint: the portion of the frozen amount for which the department says it has obtained authorization.

A freeze prevents assets from moving while they remain under a provider’s control; seizure or restraint refers here to action backed by court authorization. The Justice Department does not say that the full $1.2 billion has been transferred to government wallets or forfeited. It says the work to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets is ongoing. The Associated Press likewise reports that the government will typically file a forfeiture claim with a judge, who determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

Tracing and blocking transactions across services

The FBI publicly attributed the Binance theft to North Korea on October 31, naming the activity TraderTraitor. Its alert says the actors were converting stolen assets primarily into Ether and were expected to move them through decentralized finance and instant-swap services in further laundering steps. The bureau, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation urged virtual-asset service providers and infrastructure operators to block transactions involving listed addresses or funds derived from them, as described in the FBI’s TraderTraitor alert.

That response depends on coordination with intermediaries and services that can identify or block particular transfers. The Justice Department says the stolen assets have been laundered through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, and that it has worked with virtual-asset service providers to interdict them. The announcement does not identify the specific providers, tokens, addresses or court orders behind the $1.2 billion authorization, so it does not establish how much of that sum was held by a custodian, restrained at an exchange or moved into government control.

The distinction matters for anyone tracking the recovery. A court-authorized seizure or restraint creates a legal basis to secure assets, but it does not itself resolve ownership or complete forfeiture. The department says its action covers more than $1.2 billion of the more than $2.7 billion frozen so far; the remaining frozen funds are not described as covered by that authorization. Nor does the announcement say how much will ultimately be returned to Binance or affected customers.

The stated recovery totals therefore remain provisional measures of enforcement activity. Investigators are still tracing funds across services, while potential claimants and forfeiture proceedings determine what happens to assets secured by the government.
