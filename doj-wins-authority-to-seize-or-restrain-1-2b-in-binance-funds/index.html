DOJ wins authority to seize or restrain $1.2B in Binance funds

The DOJ says it has court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of Binance’s stolen crypto, while tracing and recovery efforts continue.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 15, 2026 · 10:42 UTC·3 min read

The Justice Department has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a legal step in an ongoing effort to freeze and recover assets linked to the hack. The Associated Press report on the FBI announcement says the government has seized the funds; the DOJ’s more specific wording is that it obtained authorization to seize or restrain them. The distinction matters: authorization is not the same as a final forfeiture or a return of funds to Binance or other claimants.

The DOJ announced the action on Nov. 12, saying the funds were part of a larger pool already frozen through cooperation between government agencies and private companies. It attributed the Binance theft to North Korean military actors it said the FBI calls TraderTraitor. The department said tracing, freezing, seizure and forfeiture work remains underway.

What the $1.2 billion figure covers

  • More than $1.2 billion: the amount for which the DOJ says it has court authorization to seize or restrain cryptocurrency. This is the legal action at the center of the announcement; it does not establish that the assets have been forfeited or returned.
  • More than $2.7 billion: the amount the DOJ says U.S. government and private-sector efforts have frozen collectively since the theft. The $1.2 billion is part of that total, not an additional recovery.
  • More than $6 billion: the value the DOJ attributes to the assets stolen from Binance on or about Oct. 29. Binance’s reported estimate, cited by The Block, was about $6.3 billion.

The DOJ says law enforcement worked with virtual asset service providers (VASPs) to interdict the stolen assets. That description points to coordination with companies able to act on assets or accounts within their services; the announcement does not specify which providers handled the freezes or how each asset was controlled. It does not identify the tokens, blockchains, addresses, or court orders covering the $1.2 billion.

Those omissions limit what can be concluded from the headline figure. The public announcement does not say that the government reversed blockchain transactions, changed a protocol rule, or recovered private keys. It describes court-authorized action and provider cooperation. The DOJ says investigators are still tracing assets as TraderTraitor actors move them through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. The Justice Department’s announcement also says the FBI had urged virtual asset providers to block transactions with, or derived from, addresses used to launder the stolen Binance assets.

Authorization leaves claims and tracing unresolved

Seizure or restraint authority does not by itself decide who ultimately owns the assets. The AP reports that the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines whether anyone has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The DOJ’s release says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the Binance assets continue. It does not announce a completed forfeiture proceeding or a distribution to victims.

The practical outcome will depend on whether investigators and service providers can identify and control assets before they move beyond the reach of those controls, and on what a court later orders. For now, the confirmed development is narrower: a court has authorized the DOJ to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion from a pool of over $2.7 billion frozen through combined public and private efforts. The amount still being traced, the assets’ composition and the ultimate disposition of any forfeited funds have not been detailed in the announcement.
