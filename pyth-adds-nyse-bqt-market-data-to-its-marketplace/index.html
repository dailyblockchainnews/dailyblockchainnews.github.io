Pyth adds NYSE BQT market data to its marketplace

Pyth now distributes NYSE BQT through its marketplace, giving licensed applications live quotes, trades and market-status data across five NYSE Group exchanges.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 12, 2026 · 04:34 UTC·3 min read

Pyth Network began distributing NYSE BQT through its Data Marketplace on Nov. 11, adding real-time quotes and trade data for U.S. stocks and ETFs from all five NYSE Group equity exchanges. The NYSE BQT announcement from Pyth describes the product as a Level 1 market-data service; it gives applications exchange-sourced market information they can display alongside securities traded on digital platforms.

NYSE BQT carries the best bid and offer, including displayed size, trades and last sale. It also includes primary-market closing prices, real-time consolidated volume and status messages for trading halts, resumptions and short-sale restrictions. The coverage includes NYSE, NYSE American, NYSE Arca, NYSE National and NYSE Texas. It extends to U.S. stocks and ETFs listed elsewhere when they trade on an NYSE Group exchange.

What does NYSE BQT add to Pyth’s marketplace?

It makes exchange quote, trade and market-status data available through Pyth’s distribution channel. In its announcement, Pyth says venues that list equity perpetual futures or tokenized stocks on Pyth Pro can show the NYSE quote and last sale next to the instrument being traded. That is a display use case described by Pyth; the announcement does not say that adding BQT automatically creates an on-chain price feed or changes how those instruments settle.

The market-status messages provide context beyond a price. A halt or short-sale restriction can affect whether and how a venue handles the related instrument. The feed carries those messages from NYSE Group, but Pyth’s announcement does not specify how individual venues must respond to them. The venue’s handling remains an implementation choice.

How do apps get the data?

Clients must license NYSE BQT directly from NYSE, then receive it through Pyth, subject to NYSE’s market-data policies and applicable agreements. Pyth’s marketplace acts as a distribution route: its announcement says institutions can make data available to trading systems, financial applications and blockchain-native markets through one integration, while publishers retain attribution and commercial control. Access through Pyth therefore does not remove the exchange’s licensing requirements.

That distinction also defines what the launch does not establish. The sources confirm a channel for licensed access to NYSE BQT; they do not claim that every Pyth user or application receives the data by default. Nor does the announcement specify feed-update intervals or a technical delivery path into any particular blockchain application.

What happens when NYSE is closed?

NYSE BQT includes the primary-market close, while Pyth says its separate Pyth Indices price U.S. equities around the clock. The company says an application can combine the exchange close with those index prices to show movement after the session and over weekends. Those are distinct data products: the BQT close is an exchange market-data value, while the after-hours pricing described here comes from Pyth Indices.

The addition follows Pyth’s earlier Nasdaq Basic distribution approval, according to The Block’s report. With both exchange groups represented in the marketplace, applications can source data from NYSE Group and Nasdaq through Pyth, while licensing and each product’s defined coverage continue to govern access and use.
