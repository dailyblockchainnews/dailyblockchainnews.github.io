DOJ gets court authority over $1.2B in Binance hack funds

The Justice Department says it has court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in Binance hack assets, within $2.7 billion frozen so far.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 14, 2026 · 23:33 UTC·3 min read

The U.S. Justice Department says it has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, as part of a wider effort to freeze assets from the October hack. The department’s November 12 announcement says government and private-sector efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion in total. That distinction matters: the $1.2 billion is included in the $2.7 billion, and authorization to seize or restrain assets does not itself establish that they have been returned to Binance or its customers.

The Justice Department says that on or about October 29, members of the North Korean military stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance. The FBI calls the actors TraderTraitor. The release does not give a breakdown of the stolen assets by token or blockchain, nor does it state how much of the theft remains outside the frozen total.

How the recovery figures relate

  • More than $6 billion stolen: the Justice Department’s figure for virtual assets taken from Binance in the attack. It describes the reported scale of the theft, not the amount now controlled by authorities.
  • More than $2.7 billion frozen: the combined result of U.S. government and private-sector efforts, according to the department. That total includes assets covered by the separate $1.2 billion figure.
  • More than $1.2 billion authorized for seizure or restraint: the portion for which the department says it obtained court authorization. The release does not split this amount between seizure and restraint.

The word “frozen” describes an interruption in the assets’ movement, not a final ruling on ownership. The department says investigators and law-enforcement partners have worked with virtual asset service providers to interdict the stolen assets. It also says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit them are continuing while TraderTraitor actors launder funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges.

That sequence is the practical mechanism behind the recovery effort: investigators trace transfers, service providers help stop assets from moving, and court authorization supports further seizure or restraint. The department’s release does not identify the court, case number, specific wallets or assets covered by the $1.2 billion authorization. It also does not say how much of that amount has been transferred into government custody. The Associated Press reported on the seizure announcement and noted that, typically, the government files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

Tracing continues across services

The FBI, U.S. Secret Service and Internal Revenue Service issued a cyber alert last week, according to the Justice Department. It urged virtual asset service providers to block transactions with or derived from cryptocurrency addresses TraderTraitor actors are using to launder the Binance assets. The release names bridges, mixers and exchanges as routes used to move the funds, but it does not say which specific services handled the assets covered by the court authorization.

The department says recovery work is ongoing. The confirmed milestone is the court authorization covering more than $1.2 billion within a broader pool of more than $2.7 billion frozen so far. The announcement does not establish how much will ultimately be forfeited, who will receive any recovered assets, or when a court will resolve competing claims.
