EU proposal targets Russian crypto networks, 60 banks

The Commission’s 22nd Russia package would extend transaction bans to about 60 banks and target crypto and payment networks, but still needs all 27 states’ approval.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 28, 2026 · 08:05 UTC·3 min read

The European Commission presented its proposed 22nd Russia sanctions package on Nov. 23, including measures aimed at crypto and payment networks. In her statement on the package, Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the EU would focus on Russian networks and third countries that help Russia evade sanctions. The proposal is not yet in force: all 27 EU member states must endorse it, and the measures could change during that process, the Associated Press reported.

For financial services, the Commission proposes extending the transaction ban to about 60 Russian banks and removing those banks from SWIFT, the messaging system used for international payments. It also says it will crack down on crypto and payment networks, including in third countries that help Russia evade sanctions. A Crypto Sanctions Task Force has been launched to support that work.

The announcement does not name the crypto platforms or payment networks covered, specify how a platform would be designated, or describe a transaction-screening mechanism. It also gives no blockchain, token, smart contract or protocol rule to be changed. The crypto measure described is directed at networks and service providers; the statement does not announce a change to how any blockchain validates or settles transactions. The scope and enforcement details therefore remain open in the public description of the proposal.

That distinction matters for crypto users and operators: the announced measure concerns sanctions against financial services and networks, rather than a protocol-level restriction that would automatically prevent a transfer on-chain. The Commission has not, in this statement, identified specific assets, addresses or platforms that would be subject to restrictions. The proposal’s practical reach will depend on the legal text ultimately agreed by member states and on which services it covers.

The crypto provisions sit within a wider package. The Commission also proposes adding more than 70 ships to sanctions listings and banning European companies from helping Russia build, operate or repair energy infrastructure, including refineries. For the first time, von der Leyen said, the proposed energy services ban would cover electricity services. The Commission is also proposing a ban on tanker vessels insured in Russia.

Other measures would bar Russian teams and athletes from competing under the Russian flag at sports events in the EU, prohibit the display of official Russian symbols at those events, and restrict financial flows from Russian sponsors to European-based sports and cultural associations. The package also proposes visa restrictions for Russian ex-combatants and would prohibit Europeans from holding governing-body posts at companies in Russia.

For now, the 22nd package is a Commission proposal, not an operative EU sanctions regime. Its bank measures, crypto provisions and other restrictions require agreement by all 27 member states before they can take effect. Until that decision, the announcement establishes the Commission’s proposed targets but does not itself impose the new transaction bans or identify which crypto services would be affected.
