Variational raises $110 million at $1.5 billion valuation

Variational raised $110 million at a $1.5 billion valuation to expand Omni markets, raise position limits and move toward round-the-clock swaps trading.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 20, 2026 · 07:03 UTC·3 min read

Variational raised $110 million in a Series B round valuing the onchain derivatives company at $1.5 billion, with the capital earmarked for new markets, higher position limits and round-the-clock swaps trading. The Reuters report carried by Euronext said Bain Capital Crypto led the round, joined by Dragonfly, HSG and Peak XV, among others.

The financing follows the expansion of Omni, Variational’s trading platform, beyond crypto derivatives into markets tied to traditional assets. The company said Omni has processed more than $475 billion in volume since launching in early 2025 and has $1.3 billion in open positions. Traditional markets now account for more than half of its daily trading volume, according to CoinDesk’s report.

Omni uses a request-for-quote model rather than matching buyers and sellers against one another on an exchange. A trader requests a price, and Variational’s in-house market maker quotes it using liquidity available through crypto exchanges and traditional financial institutions. The market maker takes the other side of every trade, according to Variational’s model as described by CoinDesk. The company says that arrangement supports more than 590 markets without a separate market maker for each product.

That structure puts liquidity provision and counterparty exposure in one place. Variational says customer collateral stays in individual onchain settlement pools instead of being transferred to outside exchanges for hedging. It also caps outstanding positions in each market to limit exposure it cannot hedge. Those caps are a risk control, not a guarantee of execution: CoinDesk reported that spreads can widen sharply in fast markets and that traders depend on the liquidity provider remaining solvent and continuing to quote under stress.

Swaps brought traditional market pricing onto Omni

Variational introduced swaps in September. The products draw on liquidity from traditional financial institutions and use financing rates tied to traditional markets; perpetual futures instead have funding costs that can fluctuate. Variational said swaps have generated more than $80 billion in volume. The new capital is intended to support their expansion toward round-the-clock trading.

Omni charges no trading commissions, but trading still carries costs. Its in-house liquidity provider earns through the bid-ask spread, while swaps incur financing costs. The quoted price and those financing charges therefore affect a trader’s result even when the platform does not charge a commission.

The planned expansion raises the platform’s capacity and product coverage, but it does not change the reported core model: the in-house provider quotes and takes the other side, collateral remains in onchain settlement pools, and per-market position caps constrain exposure. Variational has not announced a timetable for the higher limits or round-the-clock swaps trading. Its next confirmed step is to use the Series B proceeds to expand markets, raise those limits and advance the move to continuous swaps trading.
