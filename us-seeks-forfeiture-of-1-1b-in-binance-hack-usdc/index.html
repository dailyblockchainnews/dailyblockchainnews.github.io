US Seeks Forfeiture of $1.1B in Binance-Hack USDC

The Justice Department has filed to forfeit more than $1.1 billion in USDC tied to Binance’s October hack, advancing frozen assets into a court process.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 24, 2026 · 21:37 UTC·4 min read

The U.S. Justice Department filed a civil forfeiture case on Monday seeking more than $1.1 billion in USDC linked to the Binance hack. The Justice Department’s announcement says the assets had already been restrained; the filing asks a court to forfeit them. That distinction matters: a restraint blocks access while a case proceeds, while forfeiture would transfer legal ownership to the government, subject to claims and a court’s decision.

  • More than $1.1 billion: the value of USDC the government seeks to forfeit in this case.
  • $1.2 billion: the amount the Justice Department said on Nov. 12 it had court authorization to seize or restrain from the larger pool of stolen assets.
  • More than $2.9 billion: the amount the government says it has helped freeze, seize or return from multiple sources since the theft.
  • More than $6 billion: the approximate value of virtual currency taken from Binance on or about Oct. 29, according to the government.

From Circle’s access denial to a forfeiture case

The verified forfeiture complaint filed in the District of Columbia names USDC associated with 36 virtual-currency addresses across 18 blockchains as property subject to forfeiture. It alleges those assets are proceeds of, or funds laundered from, the Binance theft. The case is against the property itself, in rem; the complaint does not name an individual defendant as the owner of the funds.

The filings describe a sequence of controls, rather than a single seizure event. Circle, USDC’s issuer, voluntarily denied access to some of the USDC after the theft. The government then obtained a temporary restraining order on Nov. 12, according to its court papers. On Nov. 23, it filed the civil forfeiture complaint and sought an order continuing the restraint during the case. Circle’s access denial prevents transfers from the affected addresses; the court order supplies a legal basis to preserve the assets while the forfeiture claim is heard.

The distinction also explains the different totals in the announcements. The $1.2 billion figure covered assets the government had authority to seize or restrain as part of a response in which public and private parties had frozen more than $2.7 billion by Nov. 12. The new $1.1 billion complaint concerns a specified USDC pool within that broader effort. The government’s later figure of more than $2.9 billion includes assets frozen, seized and returned from multiple sources; it does not mean that $2.9 billion has been forfeited to the United States.

The complaint’s account of the Binance compromise

The complaint says Binance told law enforcement that an employee’s device was likely compromised after the employee downloaded malware during a purported job interview. It alleges the attackers installed an EIP-7702 injection, then used EIP-7702 authorizations to call a “sweeper” contract. That contract automatically transferred funds when they arrived at addresses under the attackers’ control. Binance reportedly indicated the attackers used this process to take control of hot wallets; this account is an allegation in a court filing, not a court finding.

Investigators traced funds across networks and through swaps, bridges and other wallets, according to the complaint. One example in the filing starts with USDe moved from Binance, then exchanged for USDC after reaching another address. The complaint also says Circle denied access to USDC at addresses identified by investigators. These steps show why a cross-chain trail does not, by itself, restore control: tracing helps identify assets, while access controls and court orders are needed to stop transfers.

The forfeiture case now puts the government’s claim before the court. The complaint sets out why the United States says the USDC is forfeitable; it does not establish that claim as a final judgment. The government says Binance recovery efforts remain part of a wider operation, and the Justice Department reported that tracing, restraint and recovery work is ongoing.
