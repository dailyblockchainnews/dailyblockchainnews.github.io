US wins authority to seize $1.2 billion from Binance theft

The Justice Department says courts authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion in Binance theft proceeds, part of $2.7 billion frozen so far.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 17, 2026 · 21:38 UTC·3 min read

U.S. authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, while a wider U.S. government and private-sector effort has frozen more than $2.7 billion. The Justice Department’s Nov. 12 announcement describes the $1.2 billion as part of the larger frozen total, not as a separate amount recovered on top of it.

  • More than $6 billion stolen: The Justice Department says North Korean military members known to the FBI as TraderTraitor stole that much in virtual assets from Binance on or about Oct. 29. The Associated Press reported the theft at about $6.3 billion.
  • More than $2.7 billion frozen: U.S. agencies and private-sector partners have collectively frozen this amount since the theft, according to the Justice Department.
  • More than $1.2 billion authorized for seizure or restraint: The department has obtained court authorization covering part of the frozen assets.

The distinction between freezing assets and obtaining court authority matters. A freeze halts movement of assets held by cooperating virtual asset service providers; it does not by itself transfer ownership to the government. The Justice Department says it has authorization to “seize or restrain” the $1.2 billion portion. The Associated Press report says the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The announcement does not say that forfeiture is complete or that funds have been returned to Binance or users.

Why the frozen total can change

The Justice Department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the stolen assets are ongoing. TraderTraitor actors continue to move assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, according to the department. These services can complicate tracing and enforcement across chains and providers, but the announcement gives no breakdown of the assets by token, chain, wallet or service, and does not detail how investigators traced particular transfers.

That leaves the figures as a snapshot of different stages in the recovery process. More than $2.7 billion is the amount frozen through collective U.S. government and private-sector work. More than $1.2 billion is the amount for which the Justice Department has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain. The announcement does not identify how much of the authorized amount has been seized into government custody, nor how much remains subject to restraint.

The case also involves attribution: the Justice Department identifies the perpetrators as members of the North Korean military and uses the FBI’s name TraderTraitor. The FBI had previously attributed the theft to that group, according to AP. The announcement reports the U.S. government’s attribution; it does not publish technical evidence about the initial compromise or transaction-level tracing behind the seizure authority.

For Binance users, the confirmed immediate result is that providers and law enforcement have blocked movement of a portion of the stolen assets, while the remaining trace-and-freeze effort continues. The $1.2 billion figure marks a court-authorized step toward forfeiture, not a final recovery accounting. The eventual amount returned, and the timeline for any return, remain unspecified in the announcement.
