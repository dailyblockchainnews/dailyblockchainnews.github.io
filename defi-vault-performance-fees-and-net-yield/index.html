DeFi vault performance fees and net yield

A performance fee in a DeFi vault takes a share of interest earned, not principal. Convert gross APY to net yield, then account for any management fee.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 28, 2026 · 01:04 UTC·4 min read

A performance fee in a DeFi vault is a cut of interest earned, so a 10% fee on 6% gross yield leaves 5.4% for depositors before other fees.

The fee rate applies to the interest, not the amount deposited. On a 1,000-unit deposit earning 60 units of interest, a 10% performance fee takes 6 units; the depositor keeps 54 units of interest, while the 1,000-unit principal is outside the fee calculation.

Morpho vaults are single-asset pools that allocate deposits across lending markets and other supported yield sources. When comparing one, inspect the Curator’s fee and allocation, and use the publication’s explanation of risk signs in Morpho vaults to assess the markets behind the yield.

How the fee changes net yield

To compare fee structures, apply the performance fee to gross yield, then account for any management fee charged on assets. The calculation is Net APY = gross APY × (1 − performance fee) − management fee, with the performance fee entered as a decimal and the management fee expressed in APY percentage points.

In the example above, 6% × (1 − 0.10) gives 5.4% net APY before a management fee. A 1% management fee would bring the estimate to 4.4%. That fee is asset-based: it can accrue even when the vault earns no yield, so a flat subtraction is a useful comparison in APY terms, while the actual charge is taken against assets.

In Morpho vaults, the Curator sets the performance fee, subject to a cap of 50% of interest. The cap limits the share of earned interest; it does not set the vault’s gross yield or tell you what the net yield will be. Morpho vaults V2 also add an optional management fee charged on assets, which is a separate charge from the performance fee.

Displayed APY is usually already net of fees. Check whether a vault’s interface reports gross or net APY before applying the formula: subtracting a fee again from a net figure understates the return. Also compare like with like, since gross APY can change as markets and rates change.

A higher-fee vault can still produce a higher net yield when its markets earn more. Take a vault earning 8% gross with a 20% performance fee: it leaves 6.4% before any management fee. A vault earning 6% gross with a 10% fee leaves 5.4%. The fee alone does not rank the options; compare net returns alongside the markets and allocation that produce them.

Pooled lenders can account for borrower interest differently. Aave takes a reserve factor from borrower interest, so depositors should compare the yield available to them after that protocol-level allocation with a vault’s net APY. The reserve factor is not the same charge as a Curator’s performance fee on vault interest.

Related questions

What is a management fee in a DeFi vault?

A management fee is a charge on vault assets, rather than a share of interest earned. Because it can accrue even when yield is zero, include it when comparing net returns and check how the vault states its rate and displayed APY.

Do Aave depositors pay a fee?

Aave’s reserve factor allocates part of borrower interest to the protocol, affecting the interest available to lenders. That differs from a vault performance fee, which takes a stated share of earned interest; compare the resulting depositor yield rather than treating the mechanisms as identical.

The net-yield calculation is a starting point, not a forecast: it shows how fees affect a given gross rate. Compare the same kind of yield figure across options, then assess whether the markets can sustain it.

Applying the fee to a deposit

Can a curator raise the fee after I deposit?

Check the vault’s current fee, its change controls, and any applicable notice or withdrawal terms before depositing. The stated cap in Morpho vaults limits the performance fee to 50% of interest, but the cap alone does not tell you whether or how a Curator can change a particular vault’s rate.

Are performance fees tax-deductible?

Tax treatment depends on your jurisdiction and circumstances. Keep records of the fee and the yield credited to you, then ask a qualified tax adviser how your local rules treat them; do not assume the fee is deductible.
