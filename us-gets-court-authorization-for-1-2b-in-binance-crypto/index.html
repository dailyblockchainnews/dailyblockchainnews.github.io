US Gets Court Authorization for $1.2B in Binance Crypto

The Justice Department says more than $1.2 billion of Binance hack proceeds is under court-authorized seizure or restraint, while tracing and recovery continue.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 16, 2026 · 12:29 UTC·3 min read

U.S. authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance in an attack the FBI attributes to North Korea’s TraderTraitor group, the Justice Department said Thursday. The Justice Department announcement describes that amount as part of a larger pool of stolen assets that law enforcement and private-sector partners have frozen.

The figures describe different stages of recovery, not interchangeable totals. The FBI said it had seized more than $1.2 billion, while the Justice Department’s account says it obtained court authorization to “seize or restrain” more than $1.2 billion of funds already frozen. The department did not say that the entire authorized amount had been transferred into government custody.

  • More than $6 billion stolen: The Justice Department says TraderTraitor actors stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about Oct. 29. The Associated Press reported the FBI’s seizure announcement and put the theft at about $6.3 billion.
  • More than $2.7 billion frozen: U.S. agencies and private-sector partners have collectively frozen this amount since the theft, according to DOJ. A freeze restricts movement or access to assets; it does not by itself establish that the government has taken ownership.
  • More than $1.2 billion subject to court authorization: This is a portion of the frozen funds for which DOJ says it has judicial authority to seize or restrain the assets.

The operational channel DOJ describes is coordination with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs. Government agencies have worked with those providers to interdict the funds, and the FBI issued a cyber alert last week urging providers to block transactions with, or derived from, addresses TraderTraitor actors are using to launder the Binance assets. DOJ says tracing, freezing, seizure and forfeiture efforts remain active as the actors move funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges.

That account points to intervention by law enforcement and service providers around assets and transactions, not a change to a blockchain’s consensus rules or a reversal of confirmed transfers. The announcement does not specify which assets or chains make up the $1.2 billion, how much was immobilized by a provider, or how much has entered government-controlled wallets. Those details matter: “frozen,” “authorized for seizure or restraint” and “recovered” describe distinct legal and operational states.

Seizure authorization is not the final ownership decision

The next legal step may involve forfeiture. AP reports that in such cases the U.S. government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. DOJ’s announcement says efforts to trace, seize and forfeit the Binance assets are ongoing; it does not report a final forfeiture judgment for this tranche.

The immediate result is therefore a court-backed route to pursue part of the frozen proceeds, alongside a broader effort to keep the remaining stolen assets from moving beyond reach. The $1.2 billion figure marks the amount covered by the authorization described by DOJ, while the larger $2.7 billion figure tracks funds frozen through combined government and private-sector action. Neither figure means the entire reported $6 billion-plus theft has been returned to Binance or its customers.
