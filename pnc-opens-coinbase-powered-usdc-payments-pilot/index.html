PNC opens Coinbase-powered USDC payments pilot

PNC’s eligible corporate clients can receive USDC on Ethereum through a Coinbase-powered pilot; payments convert 1:1 to dollars and land in designated PNC accounts.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 10, 2026 · 19:24 UTC·3 min read

PNC Bank is letting eligible Corporate & Institutional Banking clients receive USDC on Ethereum, then automatically converting the payments into dollars for deposit in designated PNC accounts. The limited pilot, announced Nov. 9, adds a stablecoin payment route for corporate clients while leaving the funds in the bank’s deposit system; Coinbase’s announcement says its Payment APIs provide the underlying infrastructure.

PNC says the service builds on its existing relationship with Coinbase and its investment in Treasury Management. It complements the bank’s faster-payment options. The companies describe faster access to funds and more efficient cross-border transactions as intended benefits, rather than publishing measured settlement times or pilot results.

How does an incoming USDC payment become a PNC deposit?

The payment arrives in USDC on the Ethereum blockchain. Coinbase Payment products power the payment infrastructure, and the incoming USDC is automatically converted at a 1-to-1 rate into U.S. dollars. Those dollars are deposited into a designated PNC account, according to PNC’s announcement.

That sequence matters for corporate treasury operations: the client can receive a blockchain-based dollar token, while the announced destination is a conventional bank deposit denominated in dollars. PNC calls the service an incoming-payment option. The announcement does not say that clients can send USDC out through the same service, keep USDC in their PNC deposit accounts, or choose a different conversion rate.

The 1-to-1 figure describes the stated conversion from USDC to dollars. It does not establish how long conversion or deposit takes, what fees apply, or whether a payment is available to use immediately after it is sent. PNC and Coinbase have not published those operational details in their announcements. The pilot is limited to eligible clients, so availability is not described as open to every PNC business customer.

What does the pilot include, and what remains unproven?

For now, the confirmed function is receipt, automatic conversion and dollar deposit. PNC says it is offering the service on a limited basis as a pilot. It has not announced a participant count, a broader rollout date, or performance data that would show how much faster funds reach a client’s account than through existing payment methods.

Faster access and greater cross-border efficiency are the companies’ stated aims. The announcements do not provide corridor-level results, settlement benchmarks or a comparison with PNC’s other payment options. Those figures would be needed to assess the service’s actual speed and cost for a particular business flow; the pilot announcement alone does not establish them.

How does this extend the Coinbase-PNC relationship?

The payment pilot adds a corporate treasury use to the companies’ earlier collaboration. The Block reported that PNC’s previous arrangement with Coinbase supported digital-asset trading for eligible private-bank clients, and that the bank began offering those clients spot bitcoin trading in December. The new pilot is aimed at PNC’s Corporate & Institutional Banking clients and concerns incoming USDC payments, rather than bitcoin trading.

For businesses, the model connects an Ethereum-based payment to a bank-account deposit through Coinbase’s payment infrastructure and PNC’s treasury services. Whether it becomes a routine alternative to existing rails will depend on details not yet reported, including practical settlement times, fees, client uptake and whether PNC expands eligibility beyond the limited pilot.
