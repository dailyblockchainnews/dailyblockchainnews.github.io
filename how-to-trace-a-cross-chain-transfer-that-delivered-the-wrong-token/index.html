How to trace a cross-chain transfer that delivered the wrong token

Which block explorer to check when a cross chain transfer looks wrong: verify what left on the source chain, then find the token and amount delivered on the destination.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 26, 2026 · 07:01 UTC·4 min read

When a finished cross-chain transfer shows the wrong result, check both the source chain explorer and the destination chain explorer; the destination record identifies what reached your address. You need the source transaction ID, your destination address and the destination chain. A completion status alone does not tell you which token the destination transaction delivered.

Trace the Bitcoin send and find the destination delivery

Start with the Bitcoin transaction, then look for the token transfer recorded on the destination chain. Those are separate transactions on separate networks, and the delivery may be submitted by a relayer or Locker rather than by your wallet.

  1. Look up the source transaction ID on mempool.space or Blockstream Explorer. Confirm the transaction is the one you initiated and review its outputs: the amount sent, the destination output associated with the transfer and its confirmation status. Bitcoin outputs show where the funds went on Bitcoin; they do not identify an ERC-20 token delivered on another chain.

  2. Open your destination address on the explorer for the chain you selected. Use Etherscan for Ethereum, Polygonscan for Polygon or BscScan for BNB Smart Chain. Check that the address and network are correct before reading the activity: an address can exist on multiple EVM chains, but each chain has its own transaction history.

  3. Switch to the address’s Token Transfers tab. Etherscan, Polygonscan and BscScan list ERC-20 transfers there separately from the address’s Transactions tab. The destination delivery is commonly submitted by a relayer or Locker, so the transaction is not sent from your wallet and may not appear as a transaction to your address. The token transfer event records your address as the recipient, which is why it appears under Token Transfers.

TeleSwap is a decentralized Bitcoin bridge that uses on-chain verification and collateralized lockers to move Bitcoin and other assets across chains. Its liquidity is part of how assets are made available for transfers; see teleswap liquidity for more on that topic.

Read the delivery logs, then match the wallet balance

Open the transaction linked from the Token Transfers row and inspect its logs. The transaction page ties the delivery to the destination chain; its event logs show which token contract emitted the transfer and the amount recorded for your address.

  1. Open the delivery transaction and read the logs for the token’s Transfer event. Confirm the recipient is your destination address, then note the token contract and amount. If the explorer lists multiple token events, use the one whose recipient matches your address; another event in the same transaction may relate to a different address or token.

  2. Compare the event’s token and amount with the asset you expected. A TeleSwap transfer that delivered teleBTC rather than the swapped token will show teleBTC’s token contract and transfer in the destination record. That evidence identifies what arrived; the completion label does not override the destination transaction’s logs.

  3. If the correct token arrived but your wallet does not display it, add the token using the contract address shown by the destination explorer, on the same destination network. Confirm the wallet is set to that network and that the contract address matches the transfer row. Token names can be similar, so use the contract address to identify the asset.

  4. Finish by comparing the wallet’s displayed balance with the amount delivered in the matching transfer event. The check is complete when the wallet shows that token on the destination chain and its balance matches the delivery amount. If the wallet still differs, recheck the recipient, token contract and event amount before treating the transfer as missing.

Related

How do I add a bridged token to my wallet manually?

Add the token on the destination network using the contract address from its Token Transfers entry. The address identifies the asset; the delivery event supplies the recipient and amount to confirm you found the right contract.

What does an event log in a transaction mean?

An event log is data emitted by a contract during a transaction. For a token transfer, the emitting contract identifies the token, while the event records the transfer details, including its recipient and amount.
