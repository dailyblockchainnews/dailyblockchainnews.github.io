Fluidkey Ties $1.6M Theft to Unpatched SigNoz Flaws

Fluidkey says flaws in its self-hosted SigNoz logger exposed session tokens used to stage $1.6 million in transfers from 52 accounts across six chains.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 22, 2026 · 07:26 UTC·3 min read

Fluidkey said an attacker used vulnerabilities in the logging tool SigNoz to steal approximately $1.6 million from 52 accounts on Nov. 19, by using exposed session tokens to prepare transfers that users’ apps later signed. The company’s incident report says the stolen funds spanned Ethereum, Base, BNB Chain, Polygon, Optimism and Arbitrum; users’ private keys remained on their devices.

Fluidkey ran SigNoz, an open-source logging tool, on its own servers. Its installation was version 0.121.1, while the two vulnerabilities had fixes in versions 0.141.1 and 0.142.1, according to the report. The company says the attacker used those flaws to read internal logs from Nov. 12 through Nov. 19, after probing the logging endpoints on Nov. 5 without retrieving log content. The Defiant’s report on the incident describes the flaws as missing authentication checks and a database-query injection vulnerability.

Session tokens staged transfers for device signing

Some Fluidkey services had written session tokens to the logs. A session token identifies a logged-in user to Fluidkey’s servers. With the tokens, the attacker created transfer requests, marked them approved and raised two-factor spending limits, the company said. Logs also contained viewing keys for some newly registered accounts.

The theft did not require the attacker to extract users’ spending keys. Fluidkey says those keys stay on each user’s device and are never sent to its servers, so a prepared transfer could not execute by itself. Instead, the attacker sent small payments that triggered notifications. When a user opened and unlocked the app, it signed the prepared transfer as it would one the user had approved. The signing step still used the device-held key; the weakness was that a session token had been enough to create and approve the request on the server.

Fluidkey says the unauthorized transfers began at 17:28 UTC on Nov. 19. An affected user first contacted support at 17:43; engineering confirmed the transfers were unauthorized at 17:54 and began shutting transaction processing down. The service was fully offline at 18:05, after which no new unauthorized transactions were executed, the company said.

The company’s review found that logs exposed personal data relating to 70 accounts, including 38 email addresses, 37 names, 17 IBANs, 24 postal addresses, four dates of birth and two phone numbers. Viewing keys for 54 newly registered accounts were also retrieved. Fluidkey says a viewing key reveals an account’s addresses, balances and transaction history, but cannot move funds.

Server authorization changed before service returned

Fluidkey restored service on Nov. 20 at 15:27 UTC with version 1.79.22. Before reopening, it ended existing authenticated sessions, updated SigNoz, rotated its credentials and removed SigNoz’s query interface from the public internet. It also stopped writing session tokens and sensitive data to logs.

The central authorization change is server-side: a transfer request must now be authorized with the user’s own key. A session token alone can no longer create and approve a request, and changes to two-factor limits are now verified on the server. Fluidkey says it has contacted affected users, engaged zeroShadow to trace funds after initial work with SEAL 911, and reported the incident to the FBI’s Internet Crime Complaint Center and the Swiss National Cyber Security Centre. Its investigation remains ongoing.
