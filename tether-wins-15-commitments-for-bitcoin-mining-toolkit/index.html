Tether wins 15 commitments for Bitcoin mining toolkit

Tether says 15 mining operators, hardware makers and pools will build MDK Workers, adapters meant to let applications work across their systems without replacing them.

The Hashbeam Desk·Oct 25, 2026 · 02:17 UTC·3 min read

Tether said 15 Bitcoin mining operators, ASIC manufacturers and mining pools have committed to build and maintain integrations for its open-source Mining Development Kit (MDK), an effort to let applications work across different mining systems through a shared interface. The announcement names participants including Bitdeer, BITMAIN, Canaan, Foundry Digital, F2Pool, Luxor Technology, MicroBT and OCEAN. Tether’s announcement of the commitments describes them as integrations under development, rather than a completed rollout.

How will an MDK Worker connect a mining system?

Each participant will build and maintain an MDK Worker, a software translation layer that connects its hardware, mining pool or system to MDK. The Worker maps that system’s existing interface to a common interface that applications built on MDK can use. Developers can then build against MDK instead of creating a separate integration for every manufacturer or pool.

The Worker exposes functions chosen by its operator. Tether says participants can keep their current technology and retain control of their systems; they do not have to replace their software or equipment. The approach standardizes how applications interact with systems, while leaving those systems free to operate differently underneath.

The need for that layer arises when a mining site combines machines from several manufacturers, pool connections and management software. Tether says those systems often do not work together, so operators manage them separately and developers repeat integration work. A Worker can make selected capabilities available through MDK, but the announcement does not specify which functions each participant will expose or when its integrations will be ready.

What has Tether shown working through MDK?

Tether says its QVAC mining agent is already operating on MDK and interacting with mining infrastructure through the shared layer. That is the company’s example of an application using MDK rather than requiring a separate connection for each system it encounters. Tether does not provide details in the announcement about the agent’s operating scope, the infrastructure it currently controls or any measured results.

The difference matters for automation: an application can only monitor or control the functions made available through a Worker. Tether says an agent could monitor machines, identify problems, optimize performance and eventually take autonomous action, but those are described as potential uses. The announcement does not claim that the 15 participants’ systems are already integrated or that an autonomous agent is managing their operations.

When will MDK reach its first stable release?

Tether said MDK would reach version 0.11 at the Plan ₿ Forum in Lugano on October 23–24, and that it targets a first stable 1.0 release by the end of 2026. Its announcement says MDK is open source now, so developers and participants can begin testing integrations and building Workers before 1.0. The release target is a plan, not a confirmed shipping date.

The commitments bring 15 organizations into the work of maintaining adapters for their own hardware, pools or systems. Whether MDK becomes useful across a substantial share of mining infrastructure depends on those Workers being built, maintained and exposing the functions applications need. Tether says the framework remains open to other manufacturers, pools, operators and developers; it has not said how many additional integrations are planned.
