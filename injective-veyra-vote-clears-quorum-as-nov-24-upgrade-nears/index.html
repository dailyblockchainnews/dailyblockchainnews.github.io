Injective Veyra vote clears quorum as Nov. 24 upgrade nears

Injective’s Veyra upgrade proposal has passed the stated turnout quorum in an active vote, setting a conditional block-height halt for v1.21.0 on November 24.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 14, 2026 · 07:59 UTC·3 min read

Injective’s Veyra mainnet upgrade proposal has crossed its stated turnout quorum while voting remains open, with activation targeted for November 24 if it passes. The Injective governance record for Proposal 717 specifies a scheduled halt at block 193,145,000, followed by a restart on the v1.21.0 Injective Chain application binary.

The proposal was submitted on November 12 and lists a voting end time of November 16 at 17:53:01. At the time of the governance record’s latest view, turnout stood at 34.76% against a 33.40% quorum: 72.40% of votes were Yes, 0.41% No, 0% Veto and 27.18% Abstain. Those figures are a live snapshot, not a final result. The proposed block height is scheduled by height, not by a fixed clock time; November 24 is the target date.

The governance message is a Cosmos SDK software-upgrade message, MsgSoftwareUpgrade. Its plan names v1.21.0 and sets height 193,145,000. If the proposal passes and the chain reaches that height, the network halts for the binary change; validators and other node operators must run the planned version for the chain to resume. The proposal does not set a wall-clock activation time. Until the vote concludes and the upgrade is carried out, the stated date and restart remain conditional.

Veyra’s proposal describes changes across Injective’s MultiVM architecture, which connects its EVM environment with the wider interchain ecosystem. It says the upgrade adds IBC interoperability capabilities intended to let assets move between Injective’s EVM and connected networks. The stated aim is to support asset issuance and access to cross-chain liquidity. The proposal does not specify particular assets, counterparties, transaction volumes or a launch schedule for products using these capabilities.

For trading, the proposal names request-for-quote (RFQ) infrastructure and expanded atomic-swap functionality. An RFQ workflow lets a trader solicit a price from a liquidity provider before execution; the proposal presents the infrastructure as support for institutional trading and settlement. Atomic swaps are designed to make the exchange of assets conditional across both sides of a trade, so settlement either completes together or does not complete. The proposal identifies these as capabilities the upgrade introduces, but does not detail supported markets, swap parameters or the systems that will use them.

The proposal also says Veyra builds on foundations established by Meridian and prepares the network for CypherOS, described as Injective’s upcoming native privacy platform. It frames confidential trading and financial transactions as part of that platform’s roadmap. Veyra’s proposal does not say that CypherOS launches with this upgrade, or specify the privacy mechanisms it will use. The distinction matters: the vote concerns the v1.21.0 software upgrade, while the privacy platform remains described as upcoming.

These planned features are presented by the proposal as infrastructure for tokenized assets across issuance, distribution, trading and settlement. Approval would authorize the scheduled software upgrade; it would not by itself establish that institutions will adopt the features or that tokenized markets will reach any particular scale. Injective’s governance record shows the vote has met the listed quorum threshold, but the final outcome depends on voting through the stated close. If approved, operators would still need to coordinate around the height-based halt and resume the chain on v1.21.0.
