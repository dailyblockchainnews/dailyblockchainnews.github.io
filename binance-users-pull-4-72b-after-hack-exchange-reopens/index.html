Binance Users Pull $4.72B After Hack, Exchange Reopens

Binance customers withdrew a net $4.72 billion after withdrawals resumed, following a $6.3 billion hot-wallet breach that froze access for about 48 hours.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 2, 2026 · 20:25 UTC·3 min read

Binance customers withdrew a net $4.72 billion from the exchange after withdrawals resumed, following a hot-wallet breach that Binance says caused about $6.3 billion in losses. The outflow is about 3% of platform assets, according to Binance, and shows how quickly users moved funds once they could withdraw again. The exchange said it would cover the stolen assets; its customers’ withdrawals reflect a separate decision about whether to keep funds on the platform. Reuters’ report on customer outflows and the exchange’s reopening puts the total at $4.72 billion net since withdrawals restarted.

What did Binance suspend, and when did withdrawals resume?

Binance suspended withdrawals for about 48 hours after the breach, then began allowing customers to withdraw again on Sunday morning, Reuters reported on November 2. Binance’s October 30 incident update says the company temporarily suspended withdrawals while it conducted security reviews, applied remediation and checked affected systems. It said deposits and other unaffected platform services remained operational. The published account describes a withdrawal suspension, not a halt to trading.

Binance said unauthorized withdrawals began at about 20:05 UTC on October 29, when its hot wallets were compromised. A hot wallet holds crypto in systems connected to the internet so it can be moved for transactions; Binance’s co-CEO Richard Teng told the Associated Press that most customer assets are kept in cold wallets and moved to hot wallets when users withdraw. The company has not published a verified account of how the attackers gained access or authorized the transfers.

What is known about the stolen assets and the attack?

Binance estimated its loss at about $6.3 billion in token assets. Its October 30 update said it had set aside more than $6.34 billion in company funds to cover the impact, including $5 billion in the same token assets that were stolen. The company said roughly $3 billion had been frozen on-chain through work with law enforcement, blockchain intelligence firms, asset issuers and industry partners. Those figures describe Binance’s response and recovery effort; they do not mean the frozen funds have been returned.

The Associated Press report on the breach cites blockchain intelligence firm TRM, which said about $6.36 billion moved out of Binance wallets across ten blockchains, with most leaving in roughly 20 minutes. TRM attributed the attack to TraderTraitor, a North Korean hacking unit. Binance said it was working with law enforcement to confirm who was responsible. The exchange also said it had found no evidence that wallet private keys were leaked, while its investigation into the attack sequence continued.

How large were the withdrawals after Binance reopened?

Reuters reported that Binance also saw $2.7 billion in customer inflows after withdrawals resumed, and that withdrawal volumes were stabilising after an initial spike. Most withdrawals were in Tether’s USDT, according to Binance. These are net flows across the platform: they combine funds leaving with funds entering, rather than measuring the total amount of withdrawal requests.

The exchange said it would absorb the financial impact of the stolen funds rather than pass those losses to users. That commitment addresses the breach itself; it does not remove the operational risk users faced while withdrawals were suspended. Binance said its security investigation was ongoing and promised a technical post-mortem. Until that account explains the additional points of breach it found, the public record does not establish how the attackers got from compromised infrastructure to unauthorized wallet transfers.
