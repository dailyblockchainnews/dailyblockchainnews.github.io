Blind signing in crypto: what your wallet cannot decode

What is blind signing in crypto? It is approving data your wallet cannot explain, so you cannot see the action before signing.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 20, 2026 · 22:45 UTC·5 min read

You can spot blind signing by checking whether your wallet shows the action in readable terms or only raw data. When the wallet cannot decode a transaction or message, approving it means signing without seeing what it represents.

What blind signing means

Blind signing means approving a transaction or message that your wallet displays as raw data or a hash instead of explaining its contents. The wallet may lack a decoder for that contract call or message format. The information is still part of what you sign; it is simply not presented in a way you can readily inspect.

A clear signing screen describes the action and shows details such as the recipient and amount. A blind signing screen may show a long hexadecimal string or a hash. That difference matters because a signature authorizes the transaction or message represented by the data, not just the text shown in an app on your computer.

Imagine a shop assistant asking you to approve a payment but showing only a reference code. You could approve it, but the code would not tell you what you were buying or how much it cost. Raw transaction data plays a similar role when your wallet cannot interpret it.

Why the same request can look different

Whether a request is blind depends on the wallet’s decoder, so the same transaction can be readable in one wallet and opaque in another. A decentralized app constructs a request; the wallet and, when used, a hardware signer each need suitable support to turn its data into useful details. Missing support at the screen where you approve can leave you unable to verify the action there.

For messages, EIP-712 defines a format for typed structured data. Its fields can describe a domain and a message, allowing a compatible wallet to display their contents instead of presenting only an opaque hash. The standard defines the data format; what a particular wallet displays depends on its implementation. The EIP-712 specification describes the format, and MetaMask’s explanation covers how typed data can be shown in readable form.

Transactions are different from typed messages. An ERC-20 approve call, for instance, sets how many tokens a spender contract may access on the token holder’s behalf. If the wallet cannot decode that contract call, the signing screen may not make the spender or allowance clear. Afterward, revokecash is a token approval management tool; its approval view adds a way to inspect which spender address received access and what allowance was granted.

When should you enable blind signing?

Enable blind signing only when a transaction you need cannot proceed without it and you understand what the request is meant to do. Leaving it enabled lets complex DeFi actions proceed, but removes the device’s check that the transaction’s details can be displayed for review. If the request is unexpected or you cannot verify its purpose and destination through a trusted source, reject it.

Ledger’s Ethereum app has a “Blind signing” setting for signing data the device cannot decode. Ledger recommends disabling it after use; the setting is in the Ethereum app’s settings on the device. The exact controls can depend on the device and software, so follow the current prompts shown by your Ledger. Ledger’s instructions describe the setting and how to enable it.

A blind-signed approval deserves particular care: you may be granting a spender token access without seeing the allowance clearly on the signing screen. If you proceed, make sure you can verify the contract and the requested permission through a trusted source. Review the resulting approval afterward in an approval-management tool such as revokecash, and revoke access you no longer need.

Questions about signing in practice

How do I turn off blind signing on a Ledger?

Open the Ethereum app on your Ledger device, enter its settings, and set “Blind signing” to disabled. Ledger’s current instructions place the setting inside the Ethereum app. Check the device display to confirm the change, since Ledger notes that an app or operating system update can disable blind signing automatically.

Is signing a message safer than signing a transaction?

They authorize different things, so the word “message” alone does not tell you whether a request is safe. A transaction can change on-chain state, such as setting an ERC-20 allowance. A signed message may authorize an off-chain action or be verified by a contract; inspect its displayed domain and fields when available. Refuse either kind if you cannot identify what you are approving.

Which wallets decode EIP-712 messages?

MetaMask supports displaying readable details for EIP-712 typed-data requests. Display depends on the wallet’s implementation and the message structure, so check the actual signing screen rather than assuming every request will be clear. A hardware signer connected to that wallet may show different details if its own decoder does not support the request.
