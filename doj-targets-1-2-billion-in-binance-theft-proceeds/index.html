DOJ targets $1.2 billion in Binance theft proceeds

The Justice Department says court authorization covers more than $1.2 billion of Binance theft proceeds, within $2.7 billion frozen as laundering continues.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 13, 2026 · 13:48 UTC·3 min read

The U.S. Justice Department has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, as efforts to trace and forfeit the assets continue. The department said in its November 12 announcement that the theft occurred on or about October 29 and that North Korean military hackers it calls TraderTraitor took more than $6 billion in virtual assets.

  • More than $6 billion stolen: the DOJ’s reported value of the Binance theft. The FBI has identified TraderTraitor as responsible, according to the department.
  • More than $2.7 billion frozen: the amount U.S. government and private-sector efforts had frozen by November 12.
  • More than $1.2 billion covered by court authorization: that amount is part of the larger frozen total and is authorized for seizure or restraint. The announcement does not say that all of it has been forfeited or returned to victims.

Authorization is not the same as final forfeiture

The figures describe different stages of recovery. A freeze prevents assets from moving while legal and investigative steps continue. The court authorization lets the government seize or restrain the specified cryptocurrency, but it does not establish that ownership has been finally transferred to the government or that victims have been repaid.

As the Associated Press reported, the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge in such cases. The judge then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The Justice Department’s announcement says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the Binance assets are still underway.

The department did not describe a single on-chain transaction or technical process that accounts for the $1.2 billion figure. Instead, it says agencies worked with virtual asset service providers to interdict the stolen assets. The announcement identifies the National Security Division’s National Security Cyber Section, the Criminal Division’s Computer Crime and Intellectual Property Section, the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the District of Columbia and law-enforcement partners as participants.

Tracing continues across bridges, mixers and exchanges

The remaining recovery work is complicated by the laundering routes cited by the department: cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. A bridge moves assets between blockchain networks; a mixer pools or rearranges transactions to obscure links between source and destination. The DOJ says TraderTraitor actors continue to use these services, but it does not identify which specific assets or services make up the court-authorized amount.

Last week, the FBI, U.S. Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation issued a cyber alert attributing the theft and urging virtual asset service providers to block transactions with, or derived from, addresses used by TraderTraitor to launder the Binance proceeds, the DOJ said. That request relies on service providers identifying relevant addresses and acting on transactions they can control; it does not by itself reverse transfers on a blockchain.

The distinction matters for the $2.7 billion total: frozen assets are not necessarily all in government custody, and the $1.2 billion authorization is not a final distribution order. The agencies say tracing and forfeiture remain in progress while the attackers continue moving funds through crypto services.
