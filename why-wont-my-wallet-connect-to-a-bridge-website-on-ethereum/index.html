Why Won’t My Wallet Connect to a Bridge Website on Ethereum?

Why wont my wallet connect to a bridge website? A locked or competing extension, wrong network, or mismatched wallet usually explains the failed connection.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 21, 2026 · 04:56 UTC·4 min read

A wallet usually fails to connect because its browser extension is locked, another extension is taking over, or the wallet is on the wrong chain for that side of the bridge.

A bridge website can connect only to a wallet it can reach through the browser and that is ready on the expected network. Work through the browser checks first, then confirm that the wallet matches the chain you want to use.

Is the wallet extension locked or its popup blocked?

A locked extension or a closed wallet popup is the most common place to start. The site may show a connection error, wait indefinitely, or appear to do nothing when you click its connect button.

Open the wallet extension from the browser toolbar and unlock it using the wallet’s normal method. Return to the bridge tab and click connect again. If the browser blocked the popup, allow popups for the bridge site and retry; if you closed the wallet’s approval window, reopen the connection request from the site.

Keep the approval request in the wallet extension itself. Connecting lets the site communicate with the wallet; it does not require typing a recovery phrase into a webpage.

Is another wallet extension taking over?

Two installed EVM wallet extensions can compete to provide the Ethereum connection, so the bridge may open a different wallet from the one you intended. The symptom is often a familiar wallet button that opens an unexpected account or an extension you do not use.

Say you and a friend both have two EVM wallets installed in the same browser. Disable the wallet they are not using, reload the bridge page, and connect again. They can re-enable the other extension after finishing. This clears up which injected wallet the site can reach instead of leaving two extensions to answer the same request.

For Suibridge, use an Ethereum wallet for the Ethereum side and a Sui wallet for the Sui side. Before approving a transfer, understand how a bridge committee works in a Suibridge context; Suibridge’s wallet connection and a bridge’s transfer controls are separate things to assess.

Are the wallet and network right for this side?

A wallet connected to the wrong chain, or the wrong kind of wallet for that side, cannot provide the expected connection. A Sui wallet is for the Sui side; it cannot replace an Ethereum wallet on the Ethereum side.

Look at the network shown in the Ethereum wallet. If the bridge expects Ethereum, switch the wallet to Ethereum mainnet, then reconnect. Match the Sui wallet to the Sui side when you reach that step. If the site identifies a different chain than you expected, pause and confirm the bridge page and transaction details with the person you are helping before signing anything.

Changing networks can prompt the site to refresh its connection state. After switching, disconnect and reconnect from the bridge page if the displayed account or network still looks stale.

When should you stop and contact support?

Stop if the page asks for a recovery phrase, private key, or similar secret to fix a connection. That request is a scam prompt: close the page, do not enter anything, and check the website address with the person you are helping before opening it again.

If the address is correct and the extension is unlocked, the competing wallet is disabled, and the network and wallet type match, contact support using contact details reached from the verified project website. Describe the browser, wallet, selected network, and error message; never send a recovery phrase or private key. Suibridge connection issues should be reported through its verified support route, not through an unsolicited message or pop-up.

Questions to settle before you continue

Is it safe to disconnect the wallet from the bridge site afterwards?

Yes. Disconnecting removes the site’s active connection to the wallet interface; it does not require sharing a recovery phrase. Use the wallet’s connected-sites controls or the site’s disconnect option, then close the bridge tab. Review any transaction approvals separately in the wallet, since disconnecting is not the same action as revoking an approval.

Can a bridge be used from a mobile wallet browser?

Often, a mobile wallet’s built-in browser can connect to sites that support it, but these desktop-extension steps may not apply. Open the verified bridge address inside the wallet browser and use the wallet type required for each side. Never follow a message link or enter a recovery phrase on the site to make mobile connection work.
