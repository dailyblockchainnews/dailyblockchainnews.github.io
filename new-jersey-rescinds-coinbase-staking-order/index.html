New Jersey Rescinds Coinbase Staking Order

New Jersey withdrew its 2023 order against Coinbase on Oct. 30, removing the state restriction on new staking while leaving future enforcement possible.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 3, 2026 · 19:06 UTC·3 min read

New Jersey’s Bureau of Securities rescinded its 2023 summary penalty and cease-and-desist order against Coinbase on Oct. 30, removing the order that blocked customers from staking new funds while the state action remained pending. Coinbase’s third-quarter filing with the SEC says the regulator notified the company of its intent to rescind the order on Oct. 29. The Bureau’s notice makes the rescission effective without prejudice and without deciding the case’s merits.

Coinbase General Counsel Molly Abraham said the action clears the way for New Jersey users to resume staking. The notice itself does not announce when Coinbase will restore access, and the company’s filing does not give a reopening date.

What did New Jersey’s order prohibit?

The June 6, 2023 order applied to Coinbase Global, Inc. and Coinbase, Inc. It directed them to pay $5 million in civil penalties and to stop offering securities in New Jersey unless they were registered, covered securities, or exempt under state law. It specifically named the “Coinbase Staking Securities.”

In July 2023, Coinbase agreed with New Jersey regulators that customers in the state would no longer be able to stake new funds while the matter remained pending, according to the company’s filing. Staking generally involves committing crypto assets to support a blockchain’s transaction validation and security in return for rewards; Coinbase facilitates the process for customers and takes a fee from rewards, according to Cointelegraph’s account of the service.

The restriction concerned new funds. The rescission removes the order that imposed it, but does not itself change any blockchain’s consensus rules or alter how staking works at the protocol level. It changes the state regulatory order governing Coinbase’s offering.

Does the rescission settle the securities dispute?

No. The Bureau’s notice says the order was rescinded “without an adjudication on the merits” and preserves the Acting Bureau Chief’s ability to bring future enforcement action for securities-law violations. The regulator has withdrawn this order; it has not made a finding that Coinbase’s staking service complied with New Jersey law.

The order’s original requirements included both the $5 million penalty and the cease-and-desist provisions. The October notice rescinds the order, but does not say whether Coinbase paid the penalty or whether any amount will be returned. It also does not describe a settlement or specify conditions for resuming the service.

What happens next for Coinbase customers?

For customers, the practical next step depends on Coinbase restoring New Jersey eligibility for new staking deposits. The company’s counsel said users could resume staking, but neither the notice nor Coinbase’s SEC filing specifies timing or which assets will be available. The state order’s removal clears a regulatory barrier; it does not guarantee immediate access through Coinbase’s products.

The filing also records enforcement actions involving other state regulators. California, Maryland, Wisconsin and Washington remain among the states pursuing matters connected to Coinbase’s staking service. New Jersey’s action therefore resolves the order in that state without resolving the broader state-level regulatory dispute.
