EU’s 22nd Russia sanctions proposal targets crypto networks

The Commission proposed targeting crypto and payment networks in its 22nd Russia sanctions package, alongside measures affecting banks, sports and energy.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 26, 2026 · 15:17 UTC·3 min read

The European Commission proposed targeting crypto and payment networks in its 22nd sanctions package against Russia, presented by President Ursula von der Leyen on Nov. 23. The Commission statement says the proposed measures would focus on Russia and third countries that help it evade existing sanctions.

The announcement does not name specific crypto platforms, payment networks, tokens or transaction types. It describes a policy target, not a published technical rule for identifying or blocking transfers. The Commission said it had launched a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support the work and align efforts with international partners.

The financial measures also propose extending the transaction ban to about 60 Russian banks and removing all 60 from SWIFT, the messaging system used for international payments. The statement does not identify the banks or specify when the proposed restrictions would take effect. The measures remain proposals: the Associated Press report says all 27 EU member countries must endorse them before they can enter into force, and the package may change during that process.

That distinction matters for crypto businesses and users. The Commission’s statement signals a planned crackdown on networks it considers involved in sanctions evasion, including entities in third countries. It does not set out a new blanket prohibition on crypto use, identify which services would be covered, or describe how authorities would determine whether a platform helps Russia evade sanctions. Those details are not in the announcement.

Beyond financial services, the package proposes expanding the ban on European companies providing services to build, operate or repair Russian energy infrastructure, including refineries. For the first time, the Commission says the ban would cover electricity services. It also proposes listing more than 70 vessels linked to Russia’s shadow fleet and banning tanker vessels insured in Russia from European ports.

The sports measures would bar Russian teams and athletes from competing under the Russian flag at sports events in the EU. The Commission also proposes banning official Russian symbols at events, restricting financial flows from Russian sponsors to European-based sports and cultural associations, and restricting Russians from holding governing roles in European sports federations.

Other proposed measures include a visa ban for Russian ex-combatants and prohibiting Europeans from holding governing-body posts at companies operating in Russia. Von der Leyen framed the package as a way to increase pressure on Russia and constrain financing for its war in Ukraine. The Commission has not published, in the statement, the implementing text needed to establish the crypto restrictions’ scope or enforcement mechanics.
