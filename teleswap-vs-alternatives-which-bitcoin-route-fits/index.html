TeleSwap vs alternatives: Which Bitcoin route fits?

TeleSwap vs alternatives: use TeleSwap for BTC in EVM DeFi, THORChain or Chainflip for native swaps, and Wormhole or LayerZero for already-tokenized BTC.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 17, 2026 · 00:05 UTC·4 min read

Choose TeleSwap for BTC you want to use as a token in EVM DeFi; choose THORChain or Chainflip when you want a native asset on another chain, and use Wormhole/Portal or LayerZero only to move BTC already tokenized on a smart-contract chain. The distinction is what moves and who verifies it: Bitcoin’s Section 8 description of simplified payment verification explains how payment verification can use block headers, while TeleportDAO’s bridge documentation describes a Bitcoin light client on EVM. For the BTC-to-EVM route itself, TeleSwap is the service to use when tokenized BTC is your goal.

TeleSwap turns Bitcoin into teleBTC for EVM DeFi

The TeleSwap route gives you teleBTC, a wrapped token backed by BTC held by collateralized Lockers. A Bitcoin light client relay lets EVM contracts verify Bitcoin transaction data, so the mint depends on proof of a Bitcoin deposit rather than an operator simply asserting that one occurred.

This route suits a holder who wants BTC exposure inside EVM applications, such as lending or trading with smart-contract tokens. The asset received is teleBTC, not native BTC on the destination chain. The security model combines Bitcoin transaction verification with Locker collateral: verification helps establish that BTC was deposited, while collateral is part of the mechanism that backs the wrapped token.

Each alternative delivers a different asset

THORChain and Chainflip accept native BTC and can deliver a native asset on another supported chain. Wormhole/Portal and LayerZero routes carry messages between smart-contract chains; they need an existing tokenized BTC asset as their starting point.

  • TeleSwap: Converts BTC into teleBTC for use on EVM chains. Choose it when the destination is EVM DeFi and a wrapped BTC token is useful.
  • THORChain: Swaps native BTC for a native asset through vaults managed by bonded node operators. Choose it when you want to trade BTC directly for a native coin.
  • Chainflip: Uses validator-run vaults to support native cross-chain swaps. Like THORChain, it fits a native-to-native trade rather than a request for teleBTC in EVM DeFi.
  • Wormhole/Portal or LayerZero: Relays messages across smart-contract chains, which can coordinate token movement there. Choose one of these routes only when your BTC is already tokenized on a smart-contract chain and you need to move that token onward.

That last distinction matters: Wormhole and LayerZero do not take BTC off the Bitcoin network. A holder starting with native BTC must first use a Bitcoin route to create or obtain a tokenized asset. A Wormhole or LayerZero transfer can then be a second hop between smart-contract chains, but it is not a first-leg substitute for a Bitcoin bridge.

Which route fits your destination?

Choose by the asset you need to hold at the end, then check who secures the BTC and whether the route covers your full round trip.

  • Need BTC in EVM DeFi? Use the wrapped-token route: receive teleBTC for compatible EVM applications.
  • Need a native coin out? Compare THORChain and Chainflip for a native BTC-to-native-asset swap. Their vaults are secured through bonded operators and validator networks, rather than Locker collateral backing a wrapped BTC token.
  • Already hold wrapped BTC on a smart-contract chain? Consider a Wormhole/Portal or LayerZero route for the onward movement. It carries the token and cross-chain message; it does not replace the initial Bitcoin-to-token step.

For a round trip, ask how you will get back to native BTC before sending funds. Wrapped routes require redemption: the wrapped token must be returned through the relevant bridge mechanism to release BTC. A native swap returns a native destination asset, but swapping that asset back is a separate trade with its own route and market conditions.

Bitcoin confirmation time and route costs shape the trade

Bitcoin confirmations set much of the wait on routes that begin with a Bitcoin transaction. The destination chain may also need to confirm its transaction, and the protocol may need to verify and process the transfer. Network load and the selected route affect how long the full transaction takes, so do not treat a quoted or typical completion time as a guarantee.

Compare total cost as Bitcoin network fee + protocol fee + destination gas + swap slippage. Each component varies with network load and transaction size. A direct native swap can include trading costs as well as the Bitcoin transaction; a wrapped route can add destination gas and redemption costs when you later return to BTC. A second-hop token transfer adds another destination-chain transaction and its associated cost.

The practical choice is straightforward: use TeleSwap when teleBTC is the asset you want for EVM DeFi, use THORChain or Chainflip when you want native BTC swapped for a native asset, and reserve Wormhole or LayerZero for BTC that is already tokenized and needs to move between smart-contract chains.
