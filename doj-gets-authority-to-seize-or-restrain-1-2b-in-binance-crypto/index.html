DOJ gets authority to seize or restrain $1.2B in Binance crypto

The Justice Department has court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of Binance’s stolen crypto, within a broader $2.7 billion freeze.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 16, 2026 · 12:58 UTC·3 min read

The US Justice Department has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, an action within a broader effort that has frozen more than $2.7 billion, the department said November 12.

The figures describe different stages, not separate pools of recovered money. In its announcement of court-authorized seizure and restraint, the department said the $1.2 billion is part of the more than $2.7 billion frozen through combined government and private-sector efforts. It did not say how much is under seizure versus restraint, or that the assets have been forfeited or returned.

  • More than $6 billion stolen: The department says North Korean military actors took virtual assets from Binance on or about October 29. The FBI’s earlier alert attributes the theft to North Korea and calls the activity TraderTraitor.
  • More than $2.7 billion frozen: The department attributes this total to combined US government and private-sector efforts to interdict the assets.
  • More than $1.2 billion under court authority: This is the portion for which the department says it has authorization to seize or restrain.

Freeze, seizure and forfeiture are separate steps

A freeze stops assets from moving; it does not by itself transfer ownership to the government. The department says it has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain a portion of the frozen funds. Seizure places property in government custody, while restraint limits its movement as legal proceedings continue. The announcement does not identify which assets fall into each category or link to the court orders behind the authorization.

Forfeiture is a further legal step that can determine whether the government may keep the property. The Justice Department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the Binance assets are ongoing. It has not announced that forfeiture is complete or that Binance, or any other claimant, has received the funds.

The scale matters, but the $2.7 billion figure should not be read as a recovery rate or as the amount available for victims. It combines government and private-sector action, and the release gives no asset-by-asset accounting. The $1.2 billion figure is a court-authorized amount within that total, not an additional sum.

FBI says laundering is still under way

The FBI says it has observed TraderTraitor actors converting stolen assets primarily into Ether. In its alert naming TraderTraitor, the bureau says it expects further laundering through decentralized finance and instant-swap services. The Justice Department separately says the actors continue to use crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges.

That movement creates an ongoing coordination problem: the FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation are asking virtual-asset service providers and other firms to block transactions involving listed addresses, or funds derived from them. The request targets activity visible to or handled by those services; the public statements do not describe the technical method used to freeze each asset or the court process for each token.

The confirmed development is therefore a new legal authority over part of the funds already frozen, alongside continued tracing and interdiction. The department has not supplied a breakdown by blockchain or token, named the court or case, or said when claimants could seek the assets. Those details will determine whether the authorized seizure or restraint leads to forfeiture and, eventually, any return.
