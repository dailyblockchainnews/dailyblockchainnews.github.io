EU proposes crypto network sanctions in 22nd Russia package

The Commission’s 22nd Russia sanctions package would widen bank transaction bans and target crypto and payment networks, but still needs unanimous approval.

The Hashbeam Desk·Nov 27, 2026 · 12:15 UTC·3 min read

The European Commission has proposed extending Russia sanctions to crypto and payment networks, including networks in third countries that help Russia evade existing measures. The package also proposes transaction bans on about 60 Russian banks and their removal from SWIFT. The Commission president’s statement on the 22nd sanctions package, delivered on 23 November, gives no names of crypto platforms or detailed rules for how the proposed restrictions would apply.

The Commission describes the crypto measures as a crackdown on “crypto and payment networks.” It says the focus will be Russia and third countries that help Russia evade EU sanctions, and that a Crypto Sanctions Task Force has been launched to support this work. The statement does not specify whether the proposed restrictions would prohibit transactions with named providers, cover particular services, or set out a process for identifying third-country networks. Those details are not confirmed in the announcement.

The bank measure is more specific at a high level: the Commission says it would extend the transaction ban to roughly 60 Russian banks and remove all 60 from SWIFT, the messaging system used for international payments, according to the Associated Press report on the proposal. The Commission has not named the banks in its statement. The crypto proposal and bank restrictions are presented as parts of the same effort to constrain Russia’s means of financing its war, but the announcement does not describe a technical link between them.

The package remains a proposal. AP reports that all 27 EU member countries must endorse it before the measures can enter into force, a process that can take weeks and may change the planned measures. The Commission’s statement therefore sets out intended policy, not restrictions already in force under this 22nd package. It does not give an effective date for the proposed crypto measures.

Other proposed financial and enforcement measures broaden the package beyond crypto. The Commission says it would restrict Russian sponsors’ financial flows to European sports and cultural associations and limit Russians from holding posts in European-based sports federations. It also proposes a ban on displaying official Russian symbols at EU sports events and says Russian teams and athletes would be barred from competing under the Russian flag. The statement does not detail how event organizers would implement those restrictions.

The package also proposes expanding an energy-services ban to cover European help in building, operating or repairing Russian energy infrastructure, including refineries, and for the first time electricity services. The Commission says it would list more than 70 ships from Russia’s shadow fleet and ban tanker vessels insured in Russia. It also proposes visa restrictions for Russian ex-combatants and a prohibition on Europeans occupying posts in the governing bodies of Russian companies.

For crypto businesses, the confirmed change is the Commission’s stated intent to target networks in Russia and third countries involved in sanctions evasion, backed by a newly launched task force. The announcement does not identify a protocol, token, exchange, wallet type or specific compliance obligation. The next substantive detail depends on the package text and the member states’ approval; until then, the scope and operation of the crypto restrictions remain unspecified.
